Why most potholes, garbage piles and broken lights stay broken

People do care. The process just gives up on them.

Who do I tell?

Roads, waste and lights belong to different offices. Most people never find the right one.

What do I write?

A formal complaint is hard to write, so people skip it.

What happened next?

After you complain, you hear nothing back.

Who is watching?

Nobody sees how long an issue has waited, so nobody feels pressure.

The problem is real. We asked people.

Preliminary findings from our own survey of 17 people and 3 interviews in Jaipur.

70.6%of the people we surveyed (12 of 17) had never complained about a road, garbage or streetlight issue.

66.7%of those who complained got no update (8 of 12 who answered).

2 of 3people we interviewed were told their issue was resolved when it was not.

Why people do not complain (13 answers)

Lack of time38.5%
Process is difficult23.1%
Did not know where23.1%
Would not help15.4%

What we heard in Jaipur

Pani Pech

Complained about garbage collection. It took several weeks to get fixed, and they were unsure which department was responsible.

Jhotwara

Complained about potholes and damaged roads near their colony. No action and no update, then a message saying the issue was resolved.

Tonk Road

Complained about a dirty, damaged footover bridge. Was told it was resolved, but still has to walk across it.

Small sample (17 responses), so treat these as early signals, not final numbers.

06 Do and don’t

Keep it recognisable

Do

  • Use white space and Cloud cards.
  • Use black text on yellow buttons.
  • Colour one word red in a headline.
  • Use green only for fixed or resolved.
  • Show real complaint data and photos.

Don’t

  • Put yellow text on white.
  • Use red or green for decoration.
  • Add gradients, neon or stock skylines.
  • Stack two yellow buttons side by side.
  • Claim features the app does not have yet.
